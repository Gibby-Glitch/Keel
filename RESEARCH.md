# Research behind Keel

A summary of the existing apps and the behavioral science that shaped Keel's design. Researched November 2026.

## The app landscape

**Drinking.** The space is led by three apps with different philosophies. *I Am Sober* is abstinence-oriented, built around day counters, daily pledges and a large community; free with a paid tier (~$9.99/mo). *Reframe* uses a neuroscience-based course and supports cutting back or quitting; reviewers praise the content but complain about subscription cost. *Sunnyside* is moderation-first and avoids addiction language, aimed at people who want to cut back (~$12–36/mo with coaching). Others: Nomo (simple multi-habit clocks), Loosid (sober social), Try Dry, Less, NHS Drink Free Days, Step Away.

**Spending.** A wave of 2026 apps targets impulse buying with one core mechanic — delay. CartPause holds items for 72 hours (and reports most get skipped); UnBuy uses 48 hours; Waity a 12-hour rule; Stop Impulse Buying: No Buy offers 24h/3d/7d. PauseBuy and Pass For Now go further and put a pause screen in front of shopping apps like Amazon, Temu and Shein.

**The gap.** Sobriety apps track drinks; pause apps track purchases. None treat them as the same pattern — an escape habit with a cost — or tie both to one savings goal. Keel does, privately, with no account or subscription.

## Methods used, and the evidence

| Method | Evidence | Where it lives in Keel |
|---|---|---|
| **Urge surfing / Mindfulness-Based Relapse Prevention** | MBRP (Univ. of Washington) teaches observing cravings as waves that peak and pass. In Bowen et al. (*JAMA Psychiatry*, 2014), MBRP and standard relapse prevention beat treatment-as-usual at 6 months; MBRP had the lowest substance use at 12 months. CU Boulder work (2023) linked even small gains in mindfulness to lower alcohol craving. | Urge tool: guided timed ride-out, intensity before/after |
| **Implementation intentions (if-then plans)** | Cooke, McEwan & Norman (*Drug and Alcohol Review*, 2023), 16 studies: if-then plans reduce weekly drinking (small effect, d ≈ −0.14), stronger when written on paper / via volitional help sheets, weaker over time; no effect on binge episodes. | Plan page; surfaced during urges; slips prompt a new plan |
| **Habit formation timeline** | Lally et al. (*Eur. J. Social Psychology*, 2010): median 66 days to automaticity, range 18–254. Early repetitions give the biggest gains; one missed day barely matters. | Front-loaded milestones, day 66 marked, slips don't erase totals |
| **Abstinence violation effect** | Marlatt & Gordon's relapse-prevention model: all-or-nothing thinking after a slip drives full relapse. | “A slip, not a reset” flow; best streak and savings preserved |
| **CBT for compulsive buying** | Mueller et al. (*J. Clin. Psychiatry*, 2008) RCT: 12-week group CBT targeting triggers, thoughts, feelings and coping reduced compulsive buying; gains held at 6 months. Kellett et al. (2021) found CBT and person-centred therapy both effective. | Thought-record journal prompts, trigger tags, insights |
| **Cooling-off periods** | Hot–cold empathy gap (Loewenstein, 2005): in an aroused state people misjudge future wants. | Pause list (24/48/72h) |
| **HALT check** | Recovery-community practice (Hungry, Angry, Lonely, Tired); a practical tool rather than a trial-tested intervention. | Urge tool, with a specific action for each |
| **Context & friction** | Habits are cue-driven (Wood & Neal, 2007); changing the environment beats in-the-moment willpower. | Trigger and time-of-day insights; “plan the drive home” reminder |
| **Replacement routines** | Habit reversal / competing-response training: keep the cue and reward, swap the behavior. | “Building” checklist on Today |
| **Expressive writing** | Pennebaker (1997) and follow-on work link writing about emotions to better mood and health. | Journal with mood, craving and ease tracking |

## Safety

Alcohol withdrawal after heavy or daily drinking can be dangerous (seizures, DTs). NIAAA and clinical sources advise talking to a doctor before stopping. Keel shows this at onboarding and links SAMHSA's National Helpline (1-800-662-4357), 988, and NIAAA's Rethinking Drinking.
